## Description of the course

This graduate course aims to provide students with advanced practical
understanding of and experience with core concepts and methods in modern
statsticial data science practice. Students will become familiar with major topics in
statistical learning, univariate and multivariate statistics, analysis of large data sets, and
Bayesian analysis. There is a particular emphasis on modeling and conceptual
understanding of statistical noise and uncertainty. Students will learn to use the
powerful statistical programming language [R](https://r-project.org).

## Assignments and evaluation

Course grades will be based on assignments and weekly reflections.
Most weeks will have homeworks, assigned on Tuesdays and due the following Tuesday.
These will be short, readable, descriptive statistical reports,
which you will write in Rmarkdown (here are some [tips for doing this](rmarkdown_tips.html)),
and so also contain the R code that produces the analyses and figures.
There will also be a few other types of assignment;
the contribution of these to the final grade will be proportional to the points assigned,
and thresholds for letter grades will be chosen to reflect a graduate-level course:
A: good, demonstrates competance in all topics and mastery of many (80%+);
B: acceptible, demonstrates competance in all topics (65%+);
C: unsatisfactory (50%+).

## Course Attendance and Engagement

In-class attendance and participation leads to better learning outcomes, and is expected from all students.
However, things happen: for instance, if you're sick, please don't come to class.
If you're going to a conference or have fieldwork, let me know.
To assist with these situations, I'll do my best to have a recorded version of class
available after the fact (and possibly concurrently on zoom, by request).
However, it's not the same thing as actually being there:
for instance, a substantial portion of class is group work and discussions.
I do like to know if you won't be there, but this is a *reason-neutral* policy:
if you don't tell me why, I won't ask.

If you are unable to turn in assignments on time,
please let me know so we can make alternative arrangements.

I understand that some students may experience an extraordinary circumstance.
In the event of such a circumstance, I will work with you to consider how we
might make adjustments to course requirements where possible. Please let me
know as soon as you are able if you experience an extraordinary event.  There
is no need to explain or offer personal information about the nature of your
emergency: I will trust you only to activate this policy in an emergency
situation.
